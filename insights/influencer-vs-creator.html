Influencer vs Creator: Why the Distinction Matters

January 2025

The industry uses "influencer" and "creator" interchangeably. For luxury hospitality brands, conflating the two is an expensive mistake.

The Influencer Model

Influencers built their following on personality. They're recognisable faces with engaged audiences who care about their lives, opinions, and recommendations. Their value proposition is reach and persuasion: they can put your brand in front of their followers and, ideally, influence purchase decisions.

This model works well for consumer goods. A trusted face holding a skincare product or wearing a fashion brand creates immediate social proof. The content itself is secondary to the endorsement.

Where Influencers Fall Short

Luxury hospitality is different. You're not selling a product that can be photographed against a bedroom wall. You're selling an experience, an atmosphere, a sense of place. The content is the product.

When an influencer with modest production skills visits your property, you get exactly that: modest content. Phone footage, basic compositions, generic captions. The reach might be impressive, but the content itself does nothing to elevate your brand. Often, it diminishes it.

Worse, that content lives forever. Long after the influencer's story has expired, their low-quality property tour remains indexed, associated with your brand, undermining the premium positioning you've spent years building.

The Creator Difference

Creators are craftspeople first. They're photographers, cinematographers, and directors who happen to have audiences. Their followers came because of the quality of their work, not despite it.

When a creator visits your property, you get two things:

  • Distribution — their audience sees your property through their lens
  • Assets — you receive content worth repurposing across your own channels

This dual value proposition transforms the economics entirely. You're not just paying for eyeballs. You're commissioning content that rivals what you'd get from a production agency, delivered to an engaged audience, for a fraction of the cost.

Identifying True Creators

Follower count tells you nothing about creative capability. Here's what to look for:

  • Portfolio quality — Can their existing work sit alongside your brand imagery?
  • Technical skill — Do they shoot on professional equipment? Do they edit with intention?
  • Visual consistency — Is there a recognisable aesthetic across their work?
  • Engagement depth — Are followers commenting on the craft, not just the personality?

A creator with 50,000 followers who shoots on cinema cameras and colour-grades their footage will deliver more brand value than an influencer with 500,000 followers shooting on an iPhone with a ring light.

The Hybrid Opportunity

The most valuable talent sits at the intersection: creators with genuine creative skill and substantial audiences. They're rare, but they exist. Finding them requires looking beyond the obvious metrics and evaluating work quality directly.

This is precisely what NUBEAUX Collective does. We don't roster influencers. We roster creators of colour with editorial-level production capabilities and authentic audiences who trust their taste.

Rethinking Your Brief

Next time you're planning an influencer campaign, ask yourself: are you looking for reach or assets? Endorsement or elevation? Impressions or imagery you'd be proud to feature on your own homepage?

If the answer is the latter, you need creators, not influencers. And the distinction is worth getting right.
